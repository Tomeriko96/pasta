# How La Carbonara sounds

Every text on the site, in every language, should sound like someone at the door of the restaurant talking to a guest. Not a brochure, and not a web page explaining itself.

## Rules

1. **Speak as "we" to "you"** (je / tu / du / vous / tú), in full sentences with a verb. "We're open every day…", not "Open daily." and not "La Carbonara is located…". Use the name La Carbonara only in titles, meta descriptions and the logo.
2. **Never describe the page.** No "Here you'll find our menu", "below", "click here", "prices in euros". If a sentence only explains the website, delete it.
3. **Name the food.** "Guanciale, egg yolk, Pecorino Romano and black pepper" beats "authentic Roman flavours".
4. **Say each fact once**, where the guest needs it.
5. **No slogans or brochure phrases.** Avoid "in the heart of", "authentic", "a taste of Italy", "nothing X, just Y", "for every occasion", "passion", and lists forced into three for rhythm.
6. **Headings name the thing**: "Our pasta", "Opening hours and directions", "Questions". Not a mood.
7. **Facts only.** No claim the owner has not confirmed. The pasta is not made in house, so never write "fresh pasta" or "homemade". Prices, hours and walking times come from the data (`${f.…}`), never typed by hand.
8. **No em-dashes (—)** and no en-dashes (–) used as a dash. Use a period, comma or colon. The build fails if one slips in.
9. **Italian dish names stay Italian** in every language (La Carbonara, Cacio e Pepe, Amatriciana, Gricia, Bruschetta…). Ingredient names like guanciale, Pecorino Romano, Parmigiano Reggiano and stracciatella stay as they are.

## Confirmed facts (2026-11-14)

- Walk-ins only, no reservations. Takeaway is possible.
- Every drink is alcohol-free (0.0%).
- Carbonara: guanciale, egg yolk, Pecorino Romano, black pepper, no cream, with linguine.
- Any pasta can be made gluten-free at a surcharge.
- Most dishes are Roman; Bolognese, pesto and quattro formaggi are from elsewhere in Italy.
- Photos: the restaurant's own plus guest photos from Google reviews (permission given, no credit line needed). Never stock, never recognisable people, never dishes that are not on the menu. Alt text describes what is on the plate.
- Not confirmed, so not on the site: fresh or homemade pasta, waiting times, group policy, payment methods, accessibility, ratings.

`en.ts` is the source. Translators rewrite in this spirit in their own language rather than translating word for word.
